# What changed our working explanation

These are technical research notes accompanying the experiments, not an article draft. They distinguish a tested result from a reason to run the next test. The [index](EXPERIMENTS.md) links the complete protocols and evidence; [Question B](QUESTION-B.md) states the larger unresolved prediction problem.

## The first distinction: an unchanged answer can hide a dependency

The original behavioral observation is a change in the later Boolean's conditional true-versus-false score margin when an explicitly irrelevant earlier Boolean changes. The preferred answer can remain unchanged. Counting copied or unchanged answers alone misses that dependence. Earlier grounding controls failed, so this is not a demonstrated ability to disable a competent verifier.

The mechanistic question is harder: why does the input edit create a particular displacement, and can we forecast its consequences using the False computation alone? A retrospective intervention can constrain that question without solving it.

## 225b: the sign and the magnitude need different explanations

The preceding factorial changed both a local gate–up interaction K and the baseline B seen by the remaining network. Either could have caused the later SiLU effect to reverse sign. The missing crossed conditions tested that ambiguity directly.

K alone reverses the tested sign in every old context; B alone never does. That supports a local causal sign account. But K alone fails the strict quantitative account in every descriptive context. The next useful question therefore concerned the baseline-dependent magnitude response, rather than another demonstration of sign reversal.

This result does not identify a learned semantic feature. K is a defined product interaction, and downstream response can depend on its computational context.

## 226b: distinguish new mixing from its transmission

Final normalization was a plausible source of the remaining magnitude difference. The finite readout decomposition leaves a median .200-nat residual-interaction effect in descriptive contexts, so final normalization alone is insufficient in this setup.

An interaction that grows across blocks might be newly generated, or an existing interaction might be transmitted differently. Norm growth alone cannot distinguish them. The finite additive-parent controls separate those terms without the disputed whole-block derivatives.

The strongest tested local reduction occurs at MLP24. Removing its newly generated term reduces both the final interaction vector and the margin gap in all 16 descriptive cases, beating matched random edits. Removing the corresponding terms at MLP27 or MLP28 instead increases the absolute margin gap in all 16. A useful explanation must preserve those different signed effects; treating every nonlinear term as the same kind of error loses information.

The first 226 run failed because of an indexing bug in state assembly. That was an implementation failure, not a falsified scientific hypothesis. The failure is preserved, and the corrected 226b replays the four reference paths exactly.

## 227b: a margin explanation can still fail the vector test

Inside MLP24, a fixed-order finite decomposition separates normalization's mixed response, SiLU mixing and a gate/up cross-product. Direct product interventions test these signed terms at the actual Both trajectory, followed by the finite suffix and head.

The product component halves the descriptive margin gap in every case, but it leaves too much final interaction-vector norm to meet the combined criterion. SiLU also contributes; only removing the combined mixed term passes both criteria. In this allocation normalization partly counteracts the gap. This is conditional normalization and multiplication geometry, not evidence for opposing learned features.

The distinction matters for Question B: improving one scalar score is weaker than predicting the displacement that generated it. The vector criterion prevented a convincing-looking product-only margin result from becoming an overbroad explanation.

The original 227 stopped because product edits and post-addition residual edits failed the frozen coordinate-equivalence comparison. The narrower 227b measures its own product interventions and still records 31/32 failed raw final-coordinate bridges. Its score bridge passes, but that does not establish coordinate equivalence. The separate local arithmetic ledger also does not certify that final bridge.

## 228: causal influence is not predictive sufficiency

The finite MLP22/23 forecast improves parent, residual and margin errors in all 32 new cases, yet fails every frozen accuracy test. This is the distinction the earlier interventions could not establish: those native writes matter, but simply evaluating them finitely at this rule's predicted incoming state does not recover the native alignment.

The trace shows direction error before MLP22. A post-observation identity check also finds an exact first-block receiving state and a 14.3% median discrepancy at the next attention update. This gives a first local boundary with a correct incoming state. It does not show which attention primitive causes the discrepancy or whether subsequent growth is new error or amplification.

A useful next split was receiver-only finite remainder versus source–receiver interaction in attention2. Experiment 229 now executes it, preserving the distinction between an algebraic decomposition, a direct causal intervention and a prospective forecast. ## 229: distinguish the first error from a behavioral bottleneck

The preceding emphasis on missing cross-source interaction was insufficient at the verdict position. With exact native incoming states, receiver-only curvature accounts for its first attention discrepancy in all 32 cases; source–receiver interaction does so in none. This localizes a remainder class, not its primitive generator. Receiver RMSNorm, QK, softmax and weight–value curvature inside that remainder are still unseparated.

Across all receiving rows, cross-source interaction is substantial, concentrated near the source. A qualified fixed-order decomposition and direct attention primitive edits account for that interaction. The position-resolved result prevents either the verdict-only or pooled-sequence explanation from being generalized to the other measurement.

Running the remaining network finitely gives only .0073 nats median margin error from this isolated early discrepancy. All cases fall below the frozen .10-nat guard, so the downstream success rule is ineligible. This is a useful boundary on the tested conditional effect; it is not evidence about the same error under 228's remaining linear transport. A diagnosis of where an approximation first becomes wrong need not identify the source of its eventual behavioral failure. ## 230–231: turn a joint local account into a forecast

The primitive factorial prevents a tempting normalization-only conclusion: it fails every case at the frozen accuracy bound, despite a large signed projection onto R. The first receiving row supplies its main normalized-input correction, carried by attention key/value paths to the verdict. The operation's product background remains consequential.

The posthoc finite normalization/product combination has much lower old-case error. Its mixed term alone worsens the normalization-only discrepancy, so that term cannot be named as a standalone explanation. The full product response in the finite-normalization background is the hypothesis frozen for 231.

On new literal pairs, all 16 forecasts meet the local native attention-2 criterion after a global pre-observation seal. This advances from retrospective decomposition to a limited prospective prediction. It still uses exact known-edit block 1 and a source-only attention call, fails the whole-receiving criterion in all 16, and never forecasts final behavior. These limits are part of the result, not deferred qualification. See [230](analysis/230-receiver-curvature-result.md) and [231](analysis/231-local-forecast-result.md).

## 232: the context of transport matters across the receiving sequence

231's verdict-row success and sequence-wide failure fit 229's position-dependent distinction: the original receiver tangent is evaluated in the False background even though the changed source is already known. 232 tests moving the expansion point to the constructed source-only state. The same finite normalization/product rule now predicts all receiving rows within the frozen criterion in 32/32 fresh literal pairs, at 1.33% median error. Linear transport in that context still fails every case, so changing the expansion point does not eliminate the need for a nonlinear correction.

A post-observation recombination keeps that conclusion precise. Source-conditioned linear transport plus the old False-context nonlinear correction meets the accuracy bounds in 32/32, at 1.51% median error. Old linear transport plus the source-context correction fails all 32. The tangent change has a much larger signed projection onto the old rule's missing vector. These are recombined sealed predictions, not new native primitive interventions or another prospective success. They suggest a separation between context-sensitive transport and a locally reusable nonlinear correction; they do not establish necessity, uniqueness or learned features.

The inputs remain known only after an exact edited block-1 computation, and the source-only attention call is finite. Derivatives at both expansion points are independently qualified with separate caches and NumPy reconstruction. All forecasts precede ordinary native True measurements. The result advances a restricted local predictor, with substantial declared arithmetic, not a speedup or an end-to-end solution. [Complete result and numerical limits](analysis/232-source-context-result.md).

## 233–234: source key/value mixing explains a useful part of transport

The change in the receiving tangent can be examined without changing the receiving input or its normalization derivative. Four derivative operands vary: source keys in the query response, the softmax Jacobian background, source values read by the probability derivative, and probability weights transmitting receiver-value changes. All 16 crossed graphs qualify against separate AD/FD. Updating keys and values together while keeping the False probability background accounts for the whole changed tangent in 32/32 old cases. Neither alone does; key-only actually worsens the local forecast. This is a restricted operator account, not a native feature identification.

The joint account includes a key/value mixed product. In the fresh frozen 234 test it predicts every whole receiving vector within the registered rule, 32/32, at 1.52% median error. Removing that product worsens every full-vector forecast and leaves 16/32 accurate. This supplies prospective evidence for its contribution to the specified predictor, beyond the earlier geometric projections.

The mixed term helps the whole vector while slightly hurting the verdict row in half the fresh cases. All row-matched random controls also preserve verdict accuracy while failing the whole-vector criterion. This reinforces the need to distinguish local vector sufficiency from an already-easy position accuracy test. None of these runs forecasts the final residual or margin. The source-only primal still uses finite probabilities; the new approximation avoids changing their derivative background, not all source softmax computation. ## 235–236: local accuracy is not compositional closure

The investigation returned to the failed full forecast rather than refining its first small error. All 47 write discrepancies plus the input cast exactly account for its parent error. A fixed signed-projection ranking selects MLP20–23. Yet the largest two discrepancies, at already-finite MLP22/23, come almost entirely from wrong incoming states. Identifying a large write error does not identify a missing local nonlinear operation.

Oracle terminal repair of the four terms improves the finite margin but fails the joint account overall. Recomputing MLP20/21 at their own changing forecast inputs then tests real upstream consequences: later states and finite writes respond. It improves median margin error 6.59→4.85 nats but has 0/32 full-response passes. An exact native reset before MLP20 still has 0/32, at 0.344 nats and 24.4% final whole-vector relative error. Only attention 21–24 remains approximate in that branch, while the fully finite control replays exactly.

These controls constrain two failure modes: the arriving state is already inaccurate, and the later attention approximations are insufficient even with native input. They do not establish unique fractions of responsibility. The earlier successful attention-2 rule is still valid, but its isolated finite-suffix behavioral effect was small; focusing only on its first detectable error did not explain the large final failure. The next mechanism test should address the late receiving attention response in this qualified reset, preserving both final vector and finite margin requirements. ## The next unresolved question

The studies now constrain one conditional propagation chain: MLP23's intervention supplies interacting displacements; MLP24 generates a consequential mixed response; multiplication explains much of its margin effect, while a complete vector account needs more. The experiments still supply previously observed parents.

We have not explained how the native Boolean edit builds the required upstream gate/up alignments, nor predicted that native response from False alone. A next hypothesis needs an explicit update and computation budget, followed by forecasts sealed before fresh True measurements. No individual component in 227b passes the combined account, and the selected late-update forecast in 228 fails on new inputs. 234 now supplies a more explicit, prospectively tested key/value account of the complete local attention-2 displacement, but its exact first-block input and untested later propagation leave the general question open. A next mechanistic test should explain a specific remaining generator or transport dependency; the evidence does not justify another broad correction sweep or a learned-circuit claim.

No priority or novelty claim is established here. Known algebra and normalization geometry can explain a local relation while leaving the endogenous computation unresolved.
